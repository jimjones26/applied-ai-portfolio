All case studies

Case studies from ~83 experiments

Each one picks a single lesson from a three-year R&D program of AI-orchestrated software builds — what broke, what held, and what it taught about where AI delivers reliable work and where it breaks.

  • Substrate — The Neighbor It Can't Retrieve

    A research desk runs RAG over one shared corpus across an ethical wall between client engagements — and the vector index ranks a forbidden document #1, because cosine distance is blind to who may see what. The desk structurally cannot retrieve it: the index only proposes opaque candidate ids, and every one is re-fetched through a tenant-partitioned, marking-gated read before a chunk reaches the model, so the #1 neighbor is re-fetched, dropped, and never enters the context. The gate is across the retrieval path, not beside the ranker. A live slice on Substrate against OWASP LLM08 (Vector & Embedding Weaknesses).

  • Substrate — The Figure It Can't Fabricate

    A market-intelligence desk lets a live LLM write its briefs — and a model states a number with total confidence whether or not it is real. The desk structurally cannot publish a figure it cannot ground: publishing is a governed Action whose eval gate scores the claim against its cited Source filing before the brief is persisted, so a figure with no authoritative source fails closed and never becomes a published row. You cannot ground against a source you do not hold. A live slice on Substrate against OWASP LLM09 (Misinformation).

  • Substrate — The Secret the Model Never Saw

    A support assistant is handed an inquiry that demands the customer's SSN, date of birth, card number, and bank secrets — a targeted extraction dressed as an “internal audit override.” It structurally cannot leak any of it: the substrate stripped the PII and dropped the secret record at context.admit, before the prompt was ever built, so there is nothing in the model's context to copy — no human approval, no output scanner. A live slice on Substrate against OWASP LLM02 (Sensitive Information Disclosure).

  • Substrate — The Injection That Can't Launder Itself

    A briefing agent is fed an untrusted source carrying an indirect prompt injection — an order to copy it verbatim into a partner briefing and exfiltrate internal notes. The agent does read it, and the injection still cannot launder itself across the trusted boundary: a briefing distilled from untrusted input is itself untrusted, the taint follows the derivation, and the egress gate refuses the delivery with no human in the loop. A live slice on Substrate against OWASP LLM01 (Prompt Injection).

  • Substrate — The Loop That Stops Itself

    A support-triage agent pointed at a flooded queue — twelve junk tickets, each a real model call, plus a prompt injection ordering the loop to run forever. The agent halts fail-closed at a declared budget, before the next model call fires, with no human in the loop and no kill switch. The injected “never stop” cannot extend the loop because the loop is the runtime’s, not the model’s. A live slice on Substrate against OWASP LLM10 (Unbounded Consumption).

  • Substrate — The Leak a Human Can't Approve

    An over-broad mailbox assistant is handed the power to forward mail out of the company, and a confidential board packet carries a prompt injection telling it to leak. The message structurally cannot be exfiltrated — refused at the egress boundary even after a human is tricked into approving the send, because classification is structural and approval does not override it. A live slice on Substrate against OWASP LLM06 (Excessive Agency).

  • Substrate — The Demand That Can't Leak

    A subrogation demand sent across the hard boundary between two insurance carriers — carrying reserve figures, settlement strategy, and a behavioral model of the counterparty, none of which may cross. The leak is unconstructable by the substrate itself; the irreversible send escalates to a human; and the receiving carrier, with no account and only a public key, verifies the demand offline. Built end to end in under 12 hours, including two new substrate capabilities.

  • Personal AI Memory: The O(history) Problem

    Six iterations of AI assistants that accumulate memory across sessions (a 15-crate Rust workspace at the latest, 404 commits, plus a deployed Python instance). The finding: AI memory isn’t a storage problem, it’s a safety-architecture problem — the store got wiped repeatedly until I added hooks to intercept irreversible commands.
